# What we need from Matty

Everything below is something only he can provide or authorise. Grouped by how much it blocks.

---

## Blocking — nothing ships without these

**1. Authorise the Kick bot.** He has to create a Kick developer app on the `mattyspinss` channel and approve the scopes `events:subscribe` and `chat:write`. Without it there is no chat verification, no coin earning, no live/offline detection — roughly half the site. Five minutes of his time, but only he can do it.

**2. Razed: a rotated key and one real API response.** The referral key needs reissuing (the current one has been pasted into a chat). Then one actual call run and the raw JSON sent over, so the field names, date handling and the `top` ceiling are known rather than guessed. Also worth asking Razed directly: does `top` go above 25, and do they offer a per-player lookup?

**3. A lawyer, engaged now.** Because the launch includes the games, someone qualified has to review the setup and produce the list of territories to geo-block, before anything goes public. This is the long-lead item — start it in week one, not week ten.

**4. The domain**, bought, with DNS access shared.

**8. A test Kick account** that isn't his, so the verification flow can be tested end to end without him having to be live.

## Content and brand

**9. Portrait** — one good photo, high resolution, ideally shot against something plain so it can be cropped.

**10. About Me** — 120 to 150 words in his own voice. When he started, what he plays, what the community is. Not marketing copy; it reads better when it sounds like him.

**11. The logo as a source file** — vector if it exists, otherwise the largest PNG available with a transparent background. What we have works but has the blue square baked in.

**12. Any existing brand art** — stream overlays, panels, banners, merch designs. Anything with his visual language in it saves guessing.

**13. Ten to twelve clip URLs** to fill the carousel at launch. An empty clips section on day one looks like a dead site.

**14. Three big wins** with the numbers attached: clip link, slot name, bet, payout, date. The multiplier calculates itself. These are the front-page hero content.

## Numbers and decisions

**15. The stream schedule** — which days, what time, and the timezone. We have 1.5 hours a day; the site needs the specifics for the countdown and the offline page.

**16. Prize tables** for the first weekly and monthly boards — the amount per rank, and how far down it pays.

**17. How prizes actually get paid** — Razed balance, crypto, or cash — and from which account. This determines what the payout record has to capture.

**18. Launch shop items and real stock.** What does he physically have to give away in month one? Merch means sizes and quantities. Better to launch with four items that exist than ten that don't.

**19. Month-one giveaway plan** — prizes and how often.

**20. VIP policy** — who gets it, how many exist, does it expire. It's 2.5× and it's his to hand out, so the rule should be his.

**21. Age gate: 18+ or 21+.** Depends on his main audience; the lawyer will have a view.

**22. A contact address for the terms.** He's the operator personally, so consumer-law terms need a real contact point. A business email is usually enough — check with the lawyer.

**23. Which responsible-gambling helpline** to list, appropriate to where most of his viewers are.
